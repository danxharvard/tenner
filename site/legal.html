Getting started

What Tenner is, how the charts work and how to find something to watch.

What is Tenner?

Tenner shows the UK’s top 10 shows and films on Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video, HBO Max, Paramount+ and Apple TV, all in one place and updated every hour.

Beyond the charts there’s New (what’s just arrived this week), Browse (everything streaming in the UK, with filters), Discover (swipe to find something new), and your Watchlist, which can tell you when something you’ve saved arrives, gets a new episode or is about to leave.

Tenner is free, has no adverts and doesn’t track you. It’s run independently, with no big company behind it, and isn’t part of any of the services it shows.

How the Top 10s are worked out

For every service except Netflix, we use popularity from TMDB (The Movie Database), which reflects what people are watching, searching for, rating and adding to their lists. We keep only the titles that are streaming on that service in the UK, with availability from JustWatch, and rank what’s left.

The Netflix chart is Netflix’s own published UK Top 10, which Netflix updates once a week.

It’s a strong guide to what’s popular right now, but apart from Netflix’s it isn’t based on official viewing figures. The arrows beside each title show how far it has moved since yesterday (you can turn them off in Settings).

How often Tenner updates

  • The charts: every hour.
  • Netflix’s Top 10: weekly, when Netflix publishes it.
  • Browse: every six hours.
  • New this week: once a day.
  • Tenner TV’s listings: every three hours.

It all runs by itself, and a watchdog checks every hour that nothing has fallen behind. You can see how fresh everything is right now on System status.

Add Tenner to your Home Screen

Tenner works like an app once it’s on your Home Screen or Dock: it opens full screen, starts instantly and works offline.

  • iPhone and iPad: open tenner.stream in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Mac: in Safari, choose File › Add to Dock.
  • Chrome and Edge on a computer: click the install icon at the right of the address bar.
  • Android: open the browser’s menu and choose Install app or Add to Home screen.

On iPhone and iPad, this is also what lets Tenner send you Watchlist alerts.

Search, Browse and Discover

Search finds any film, show or person, not just what’s in the charts. Press / or ⌘K (CtrlK on Windows) from any page to start typing.

Browse is everything streaming in the UK. Filter by service, genre, year and more; your filters live in the page’s address, so you can bookmark or share them.

Discover shows you one title at a time: swipe right on what you like, left on what you don’t. The more you swipe, the better For you gets at suggesting things. Your swipes stay on your device.

Your Watchlist and progress

Tap the bookmark on any title to save it to your Watchlist. For shows, you can set the season and episode you’re up to, and Tenner will show you what’s next.

Without an account, your Watchlist stays in this browser on this device. Sign in to keep it in step on all your devices, or move it across without one.

You can also share your Watchlist as a link from the Account page; anyone with the link can see it until you turn sharing off.

Choose your services

Tell Tenner which services you pay for and Browse, Discover and the homepage’s rows can stick to what you can actually watch.

Go to Settings › Charts › My services, and switch on the ones you have. It’s saved on your device, and in your account if you sign in.

Tenner TV

Tenner TV is Tenner’s Freeview guide: what’s on every channel today and over the coming days, updated every three hours. It opens instantly and works offline.

Sign in on Tenner TV with your Tenner account and your pinned channels and reminders are kept with it, and your Watchlist appears on its own shelf. Title pages on tenner.stream also show when something’s on Freeview this week.

Account and sign-in

Signing in, passkeys, passwords, devices and deleting your account.

Do I need an account?

No. Everything in Tenner works without one, and your Watchlist and settings are kept on your device.

An account is free, and keeps your Watchlist, progress, services, notes and settings in step on every phone and computer you use. It’s also what the Sunday email and Tenner TV’s sign-in use. Sign in or sign up.

Ways to sign in

  • A passkey: the quickest and safest. Your device unlocks it with Face ID, Touch ID or its screen lock. More about passkeys.
  • Google: sign in with your Google account.
  • Email and password: we’ll email you a link to confirm your address when you sign up.

You can link more than one to the same account on the Account page. Turn off Remember me as you sign in on a shared computer and Tenner signs you out once you’ve closed it.

Passkeys

A passkey replaces a password with your device’s own lock (Face ID, Touch ID, a fingerprint or your PIN). It can’t be guessed, reused or phished.

Only the public half of a passkey is kept by Tenner; the private half never leaves your device or password manager (such as iCloud Keychain or Google Password Manager), which also keeps it in step on your other devices.

Add one from the Account page once you’re signed in.

Forgotten your password

  1. Open Sign in and type your email address.
  2. Tap Forgot password?
  3. We’ll email you a link to choose a new one. Check your junk folder if it doesn’t arrive within a few minutes.

If you signed up with Google or a passkey, there’s no Tenner password to reset: just sign in that way again.

See and sign out your devices

The Account page lists every device you’re signed in on, with when it was last used. Tap Sign out beside any of them and it stops working straight away, which is handy if you’ve lost a phone or used someone else’s computer.

Move to another device without an account

In Settings › Your data, choose Move to another device. Scan the code with the other phone’s camera, or open the link there, and your Watchlist, where you’re up to and your services come with it.

Nothing is uploaded: everything is inside the link itself. You can also Export a file and import it on the other device.

Import from Letterboxd, IMDb, Trakt or Netflix

In Settings › Your data, choose Import from a file and pick your export from Letterboxd, IMDb, Trakt or your Netflix viewing history (or a Tenner export). It adds to what’s already there.

The file is read on your device. Only the names, years and IMDb numbers of its titles are sent to Tenner’s search service to find each one; the file itself is never uploaded or kept.

Delete your account

Open the Account page, scroll to the bottom and choose Delete account. If your account is linked to Google, unlink Google first (Tenner offers to do it for you). Tenner asks once to make sure, and then everything kept with your account is removed straight away: your name, sign-in methods, Watchlist, notes, settings and devices.

Your Watchlist, progress and services are cleared from this device too, and from your other devices the next time they open Tenner. Your email and Google account are free to use for a new Tenner account straight away. If you can’t sign in any more, contact us and we’ll delete it for you.

Alerts and email

Watchlist alerts, quiet hours and the Sunday email.

Turn on Watchlist alerts

Go to Settings › Alerts and switch on Watchlist alerts, then allow notifications when your browser asks.

Under Alert me about you can choose what you hear about: arrivals and new seasons, new episodes out today, titles leaving soon, Watchlist titles on Freeview today, and ones that got cheaper to rent or buy. Quiet hours hold arrivals until the morning.

Alerts are tied to this device. Turn them on on each device you want them on.

Alerts on iPhone and iPad

Apple only lets websites send notifications once they’ve been added to the Home Screen. So:

  1. Add Tenner to your Home Screen from Safari.
  2. Open Tenner from its new icon.
  3. Go to Settings › Alerts and switch on Watchlist alerts.

You’ll need iOS or iPadOS 16.4 or later.

Not getting alerts?

  • Check notifications are allowed for Tenner (or your browser) in your device’s settings, and that a Focus or Do Not Disturb isn’t holding them.
  • Check Alert me about and Quiet hours in Tenner’s Settings.
  • On iPhone and iPad, open Tenner from its Home Screen icon, not Safari (see Alerts on iPhone and iPad).
  • Try switching Watchlist alerts off and on again. This gives your device a fresh push address.

Still nothing? Report a problem and include your device details.

The Sunday email

A short round-up each Sunday morning of new episodes, arrivals and what’s leaving from your Watchlist. It needs an account: sign in, then switch on Sunday email in Settings › Alerts.

To stop it, use the unsubscribe link at the bottom of any email, or switch it off in Settings. Nothing else is ever sent to that address apart from account emails you ask for (like a password reset).

Fixing problems

When something doesn’t look right, start here.

The charts look out of date

  1. Check System status to see when the charts were last updated.
  2. If they’re fresh there but not on your screen, pull down to refresh (or reload the page). Tenner keeps an offline copy so it opens quickly, and it fetches the latest charts as it opens.
  3. Remember that Netflix’s chart only changes once a week, when Netflix publishes it.

Get the latest version of Tenner

Tenner updates itself: the next time you open it after an update, the new version is fetched in the background. The build number at the bottom of the menu shows which version you’re on, and the Update centre shows what’s new.

If something seems stuck, close every Tenner tab (or the Home Screen app) and open it again. As a last resort, clear the website data for tenner.stream in your browser’s settings.

Before you clear website data: if you don’t have an account, this also removes your Watchlist and settings. Export them first, or sign in.

A title’s details or where to watch are wrong

Title details, artwork and where to watch come from TMDB and JustWatch, and what’s on each service can change without notice. A change usually reaches Tenner within a day.

If something’s still wrong, tell us which title and what you expected to see. You can also correct details yourself on TMDB, which helps everyone who uses it.

Trailers won’t play

Trailers come from YouTube, in its privacy-enhanced mode. Content blockers and some school or work networks block it.

Try pausing your blocker for tenner.stream, or set Play trailers to On YouTube in Settings › Titles to open them there instead.

Report a problem

The more we know, the quicker we can fix it. Tell us:

  • what you were doing, and what happened instead;
  • which page or title it was on;
  • your device and browser. Your device collects these for you and can add them to your message.

Privacy Policy

1. Overview

Tenner (“we”, “us”, “our”) is a website that shows the UK’s most popular shows and films on Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video, HBO Max, Paramount+ and Apple TV, updated every hour. This policy explains what information is involved when you visit the site and what choices you have.

The short version: unless you choose to create an account, we don’t collect or store any personal information about you. There’s no advertising and no tracking. Accounts are optional, and so are messages and Watchlist alerts; each is explained below.

2. Information we collect

We don’t collect personal information when you visit the site. In particular, the site does not use:

  • analytics or visitor-tracking tools;
  • advertising or advertising networks;
  • cookies of any kind, including for accounts (see Cookies).

We don’t ask for your location. The charts are for the UK as a whole.

If you send us a message. The Contact page’s form sends your message, your email address, the type of enquiry you choose and your name, if you add it, to us as an email, through our search service on Cloudflare. If you choose to add your device details, those go too (the browser, system, screen size and Tenner version listed on Your device). It isn’t stored there: it’s passed straight on to our inbox. If you email us at support@tenner.stream instead, we receive your email address and whatever you include in your message. We use it only to read and reply to you. We don’t add you to any mailing list or share your details with anyone. Emails are kept only as long as needed to deal with your message, and you can ask us to delete them at any time. Our email provider processes messages under its own privacy policy.

If you turn on Watchlist alerts. Alerts are off unless you switch them on. When you do, your browser gives Tenner a push address for this device (made by your browser’s own notification service, such as Apple’s, Google’s or Mozilla’s), and Tenner keeps it on Cloudflare together with the list of titles on your Watchlist (their TMDB numbers only), so it can tell you when one of them arrives on a service, gets a new season or episode, or is about to leave. Nothing else is kept: no name, email address or location. The list is updated when your Watchlist changes, and both are deleted when you turn alerts off, or when your browser tells us the address no longer works. Alerts are sent through your browser’s notification service and are encrypted so that only your device can read them.

If you turn on the Sunday email. It’s off unless you switch it on in Settings while signed in. Tenner then sends one email each Sunday to your account’s confirmed email address (the one you signed up with, or your Google account’s), using the Watchlist and services already kept with your account. Every email has an unsubscribe link, and switching it off in Settings works too. Emails are sent through Resend.

If you import from another app. When you import a Letterboxd, IMDb, Trakt or Netflix file, it’s read on your device. Only the names, years and IMDb numbers of the titles in it are sent to Tenner’s search service to find each one; the file itself is never uploaded or kept.

If you create an account. Accounts are optional; Tenner works the same without one. If you make one, we keep:

  • Your name, as you typed it (or your first name from Google), and the colour you choose for your initial;
  • How you sign in: the public half of each passkey you add (the private half never leaves your device or password manager); if you use Google, Google’s number for your account, your email address and your profile picture’s address; if you use an email and password, your email address and a scrambled copy of your password (it’s scrambled on your device before it’s sent, and again by us, so we never see or keep the password itself). We keep these only to recognise you, show which accounts are linked and, if you ask, email you a link to reset your password;
  • What you keep in Tenner: your Watchlist, the titles you’ve loved, which way you swiped titles in Discover, where you’re up to in shows, what you’ve marked as watched or not interested, your notes on titles (only you can see them), your services and your settings, and Tenner TV’s pinned channels and reminders, so they’re the same on every device you sign in on;
  • Where you’re signed in: the kind of device (such as “iPhone”), when you signed in and when it was last used. The sign-in itself is a random code kept on your device; we store only a scrambled copy of it;
  • Your shared list link, if you turn sharing on. Anyone with the link can see your name, picture and Watchlist until you turn it off.

This is kept in a database run by Cloudflare. We use it only to run your account. You can see it on the Account page, sign out of any device, and delete your account there at any time, which removes everything above straight away. Accounts don’t use cookies: how this device stays signed in is explained in section 3.

3. Information stored on your device

To make the site work better, your browser keeps a few things on your own device:

  • Your theme: if you pick Auto or Day instead of Night.
  • Shows, Films or Both: which charts you last chose to see.
  • An offline copy: the site’s pages, its latest charts and some recent posters, so Tenner opens quickly and still works without a connection (especially if you add it to your Home Screen).
  • Your Watchlist: the titles you save, with their names and posters. It stays in this browser, on this device, unless you sign in to an account (which keeps it in step on your devices), or carry it across yourself with Move to another device in Settings.
  • My services: the streaming services you’ve told Tenner you use, if you choose them, so Browse, Discover and the homepage’s rows can stick to what you can watch.
  • Your settings: the choices you make on the Settings page, such as which page Tenner opens on.
  • Your progress: the season and episode you’re up to in shows you’re watching, if you set it. Like your Watchlist, it stays on this device.
  • Discover: the titles you’ve swiped past, so they don’t come round again (up to the 3,000 most recent), whether you last chose shows, films or both, and which way you swiped (the 1,500 most recent), used only to suggest things you might like in For you.
  • Browse: whether you like posters or a list, and that you’ve seen the note about Browse changing.
  • Suggestions and tips: which of Tenner’s suggestion cards and feature tips you’ve closed or seen, so they stay closed, and, for the length of a visit, whether you’re seeing one.
  • Hearts: the shows and films you’ve loved, used only to suggest things you might like in For you.
  • A message you’re writing: what you’ve typed on the Contact page, for the length of a visit, so leaving the page doesn’t lose it.
  • Your sign-in, if you have an account: the random code that keeps this device signed in, a copy of your name, picture, email address and colour so the menu can show who’s signed in straight away, and when your Watchlist, services and settings last changed, so your devices stay in step. If you turn off Remember me as you sign in, this device also keeps a note of that and of when Tenner was last open, so it can sign you out once you’ve closed it. They’re removed when you sign out (and you can choose to remove your Watchlist and the rest from the device too), or when you’re signed out from another device.
  • Passkeys, if you add one: these are kept by your device or password manager, not by Tenner.
  • The privacy note: that you’ve pressed Got it, so the note about cookies isn’t shown again.

Your filters and searches on Browse live only in the page’s address, so you can bookmark or share them; they aren’t saved anywhere. Searches in this Help Centre happen on your device and aren’t sent anywhere. Move to another device (in Settings) puts your Watchlist, progress and services into a link or QR code that only you see; nothing is uploaded. Apart from Watchlist alerts and your account, if you choose them (above), none of this is ever sent to us or anyone else. You can remove it at any time by clearing your browser’s data for this site.

4. Third-party services

Like every website, Tenner relies on a few outside services. When your browser connects to them, they receive the technical details that any web request carries, such as your IP address and browser type. Each handles that information under its own privacy policy:

  • Cloudflare hosts the website and its hourly charts, and runs Tenner’s search service. Cloudflare may log visitors’ IP addresses for security, to keep the service running and to turn away automated traffic. When you search all films, shows and people, or open a cast list, what you typed or opened is passed to TMDB to find the answer. The search service also passes on messages from the Contact page to our inbox, keeps the push address and Watchlist of anyone who turns on alerts, and holds accounts for anyone who makes one (see section 2). Nothing else is stored against you. See Cloudflare’s Privacy Policy.
  • GitHub runs the automated process that gathers the charts every hour. Your browser doesn’t contact GitHub while you use Tenner. See GitHub’s Privacy Statement.
  • TMDB (The Movie Database) supplies the posters, artwork and service logos, which your browser loads directly from TMDB’s image servers. See TMDB’s Privacy Policy.
  • Google provides Sign in with Google. Its button loads from Google only on the Account page, and only Google’s sign-in is involved: if you use it, Google tells us your account number, name, email address and picture. See Google’s Privacy Policy.
  • Resend sends our emails (the link to confirm your email address when you sign up with a password, the link to reset your password, and the Sunday email if you turn it on): it receives your email address and that email. See Resend’s Privacy Policy.
  • YouTube plays trailers. On a computer, a title’s trailer starts by itself (with the sound off) a moment after you open it; on phones, and if you turn off “Start trailers by themselves” in Settings, only when you press Trailer. Tenner uses YouTube’s privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com), and nothing loads from YouTube until a trailer plays. See Google’s Privacy Policy.

The charts and where-to-watch details are gathered by our own automated process from TMDB (with where-to-watch data from JustWatch), Netflix’s published Top 10, Watchmode and Wikidata. Your browser doesn’t contact JustWatch, Netflix, Watchmode or Wikidata while you use Tenner. Watchmode also supplies the Coming soon and Leaving soon dates and the rent and buy prices. If you follow a link to Netflix, TMDB or another site, that site’s own policies apply.

Cookies

Tenner doesn’t set any cookies, and doesn’t use any third-party cookies for tracking or advertising, so there’s nothing to accept or turn off. The things listed in section 3 of the Privacy Policy are kept in your browser’s own storage on this device, not in cookies.

Accounts

Accounts don’t use cookies either. When you sign in, this device is given a random code that’s kept in your browser’s storage and sent with Tenner’s own requests to its account service, so it knows it’s you; we store only a scrambled copy of it. It isn’t shared with anyone and can’t follow you to other websites. Signing out, or signing a device out from the Account page, makes it stop working straight away.

Sign in with Google

Google’s button only loads on the Account page. Google may use its own cookies, on its own site, to know whether you’re signed in to Google and to keep its sign-in secure. Those are Google’s, under Google’s Privacy Policy; Tenner can’t read them. If you sign in with a passkey or an email and password, Google isn’t involved.

Trailers

Trailers play in YouTube’s privacy-enhanced mode, which doesn’t set cookies until a trailer plays, and nothing loads from YouTube before then.

Cloudflare

Cloudflare, which hosts Tenner, may set a strictly necessary security cookie of its own if it needs to check a visit is from a person rather than an automated program. It isn’t used for tracking or advertising.

Apart from your account and Watchlist alerts, if you choose them, what Tenner keeps on your device never leaves it.

See, download or delete your data

  • See it: everything kept with your account is shown on the Account page.
  • Take a copy: Settings › Your data › More › Export Watchlist and progress saves a file you can keep or import elsewhere.
  • Correct it: change your name, colour and sign-in methods on the Account page.
  • Delete it: delete your account, turn alerts off (which deletes the push address), or clear this device with Settings › Your data › Clear all.

For anything else, including a full copy of what we hold or deleting a message you sent us, contact us with the Legal topic. We’ll reply within a month, and usually much sooner.

Security and reporting a vulnerability

How Tenner keeps your account safe:

  • Passwords are scrambled on your device before they’re sent, and again by us, so we never see or keep the password itself. Passkeys are better still: nothing secret ever leaves your device.
  • Sign-ins are random codes; we keep only a scrambled copy, and you can sign out any device from the Account page.
  • Everything travels over encrypted connections, and Watchlist alerts are encrypted so only your device can read them.
  • The site keeps no secrets in its code; the keys it needs are kept by the services that run it.

Found a security problem? Please tell us privately on the Contact page (choose Legal) or at support@tenner.stream, with the steps to reproduce it. Please don’t access other people’s data, disrupt the service or share the problem publicly until it’s fixed. We’ll reply quickly, keep you posted and thank you once it’s fixed.

Children and age ratings

Tenner is a general-audience site, and the charts include whatever is popular, which can include titles for adults.

To keep them out of sight, switch on Hide titles rated 18 in Settings › Titles › More. For stronger controls, use each streaming service’s own parental controls and profiles.

Accessibility

We want Tenner to work well for everyone. It follows your device’s settings:

  • Dark Mode or light, or your own choice of Day, Night or Auto in the Theme menu;
  • Reduce Motion: animations stop, and they can be switched off in Settings › Appearance too;
  • Reduce Transparency: frosted glass becomes solid;
  • Larger text and zoom, without anything running off the page;
  • VoiceOver and other screen readers: every button and icon has a label, and there’s a link to skip straight to the page;
  • Keyboard: everything can be reached with Tab, and there are shortcuts for the most common things.

Something hard to use? Tell us and we’ll put it right.

How Tenner is built

  • A fast, light website: plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with no frameworks, served by Cloudflare from close to you.
  • Charts on the hour: an automated process on GitHub Actions gathers the charts, Browse and title details, and puts the new versions live by itself.
  • Search and accounts: a small service on Cloudflare Workers, with accounts kept in Cloudflare’s database.
  • Works offline: a service worker keeps the pages and the latest charts on your device.
  • Looks after itself: a watchdog checks the site, the charts’ age, search and Tenner TV every hour, and re-runs anything that’s behind.

Supported browsers and devices

Tenner works on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Android phones and tablets, Windows and Linux, in the current versions of:

  • Safari (iOS and iPadOS 16.4 or later for alerts);
  • Chrome, Edge and other Chromium browsers;
  • Firefox.

Older browsers may still show the charts, but some features (like passkeys, alerts and the smoothest animations) need a recent one.
