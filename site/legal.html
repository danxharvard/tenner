Privacy Policy

Updated 17 November 2026

1. Overview

Tenner (“we”, “us”, “our”) is a website that shows the UK’s most popular shows and films on Netflix, Disney+, Prime Video, HBO Max, Paramount+ and Apple TV, updated every hour. This policy explains what information is involved when you visit the site and what choices you have.

The short version: unless you choose to create an account, we don’t collect or store any personal information about you. There’s no advertising and no tracking. Accounts are optional, and so are emails to us and Watchlist alerts; each is explained below.

2. Information we collect

We don’t collect personal information when you visit the site. In particular, the site does not use:

  • analytics or visitor-tracking tools;
  • advertising or advertising networks;
  • cookies of any kind, including for accounts (see Cookies).

We don’t ask for your location. The charts are for the UK as a whole.

If you email us. When you write to support@tenner.stream, we receive your email address and whatever you include in your message. We use it only to read and reply to you. We don’t add you to any mailing list or share your details with anyone. Emails are kept only as long as needed to deal with your message, and you can ask us to delete them at any time. Our email provider processes messages under its own privacy policy.

If you turn on Watchlist alerts. Alerts are off unless you switch them on. When you do, your browser gives Tenner a push address for this device (made by your browser’s own notification service, such as Apple’s, Google’s or Mozilla’s), and Tenner keeps it on Cloudflare together with the list of titles on your Watchlist (their TMDB numbers only), so it can tell you when one of them arrives on a service, gets a new season or episode, or is about to leave. Nothing else is kept: no name, email address or location. The list is updated when your Watchlist changes, and both are deleted when you turn alerts off, or when your browser tells us the address no longer works. Alerts are sent through your browser’s notification service and are encrypted so that only your device can read them.

If you turn on the Sunday email. It’s off unless you switch it on in Settings while signed in. Tenner then sends one email each Sunday to your account’s confirmed email address (the one you signed up with, or your Google account’s), using the Watchlist and services already kept with your account. Every email has an unsubscribe link, and switching it off in Settings works too. Emails are sent through Resend.

If you import from another app. When you import a Letterboxd, IMDb, Trakt or Netflix file, it’s read on your device. Only the names, years and IMDb numbers of the titles in it are sent to Tenner’s search service to find each one; the file itself is never uploaded or kept.

If you create an account. Accounts are optional; Tenner works the same without one. If you make one, we keep:

  • Your name, as you typed it (or your first name from Google), and the colour you choose for your initial;
  • How you sign in: the public half of each passkey you add (the private half never leaves your device or password manager); if you use Google, Google’s number for your account, your email address and your profile picture’s address; if you use an email and password, your email address and a scrambled copy of your password (it’s scrambled on your device before it’s sent, and again by us, so we never see or keep the password itself). We keep these only to recognise you, show which accounts are linked and, if you ask, email you a link to reset your password;
  • What you keep in Tenner: your Watchlist, the titles you’ve loved, which way you swiped titles in Discover, where you’re up to in shows, what you’ve marked as watched or not interested, your notes on titles (only you can see them), your services and your settings, and Tenner TV’s pinned channels and reminders, so they’re the same on every device you sign in on;
  • Where you’re signed in: the kind of device (such as “iPhone”), when you signed in and when it was last used. The sign-in itself is a random code kept on your device; we store only a scrambled copy of it;
  • Your shared list link, if you turn sharing on. Anyone with the link can see your name, picture and Watchlist until you turn it off.

This is kept in a database run by Cloudflare. We use it only to run your account. You can see it on the Account page, sign out of any device, and delete your account there at any time, which removes everything above straight away. Accounts don’t use cookies: how this device stays signed in is explained in section 3.

3. Information stored on your device

To make the site work better, your browser keeps a few things on your own device:

  • Your theme: if you pick Auto or Day instead of Night.
  • Shows, Films or Both: which charts you last chose to see.
  • An offline copy: the site’s pages, its latest charts and some recent posters, so Tenner opens quickly and still works without a connection (especially if you add it to your Home Screen).
  • Your Watchlist: the titles you save, with their names and posters. It stays in this browser, on this device, unless you sign in to an account (which keeps it in step on your devices), or carry it across yourself with Move to another device in Settings.
  • My services: the streaming services you’ve told Tenner you use, if you choose them, so Browse, Discover and the homepage’s rows can stick to what you can watch.
  • Your settings: the choices you make on the Settings page, such as which page Tenner opens on.
  • Your progress: the season and episode you’re up to in shows you’re watching, if you set it. Like your Watchlist, it stays on this device.
  • Discover: the titles you’ve swiped past, so they don’t come round again (up to the 3,000 most recent), whether you last chose shows, films or both, and which way you swiped (the 1,500 most recent), used only to suggest things you might like in For you.
  • Browse: whether you like posters or a list, and that you’ve seen the note about Browse changing.
  • Suggestions and tips: which of Tenner’s suggestion cards and feature tips you’ve closed or seen, so they stay closed, and, for the length of a visit, whether you’re seeing one.
  • Hearts: the shows and films you’ve loved, used only to suggest things you might like in For you.
  • Your sign-in, if you have an account: the random code that keeps this device signed in, a copy of your name, picture, email address and colour so the menu can show who’s signed in straight away, and when your Watchlist, services and settings last changed, so your devices stay in step. If you turn off Remember me as you sign in, this device also keeps a note of that and of when Tenner was last open, so it can sign you out once you’ve closed it. They’re removed when you sign out (and you can choose to remove your Watchlist and the rest from the device too), or when you’re signed out from another device.
  • Passkeys, if you add one: these are kept by your device or password manager, not by Tenner.
  • The privacy note: that you’ve pressed Got it, so the note about cookies isn’t shown again.

Your filters and searches on Browse live only in the page’s address, so you can bookmark or share them; they aren’t saved anywhere. Move to another device (in Settings) puts your Watchlist, progress and services into a link or QR code that only you see; nothing is uploaded. Apart from Watchlist alerts and your account, if you choose them (above), none of this is ever sent to us or anyone else. You can remove it at any time by clearing your browser’s data for this site.

4. Third-party services

Like every website, Tenner relies on a few outside services. When your browser connects to them, they receive the technical details that any web request carries, such as your IP address and browser type. Each handles that information under its own privacy policy:

  • Cloudflare hosts the website and its hourly charts, and runs Tenner’s search service. Cloudflare may log visitors’ IP addresses for security, to keep the service running and to turn away automated traffic. When you search all films, shows and people, or open a cast list, what you typed or opened is passed to TMDB to find the answer. The search service also keeps the push address and Watchlist of anyone who turns on alerts, and holds accounts for anyone who makes one (see section 2). Nothing else is stored against you. See Cloudflare’s Privacy Policy.
  • GitHub runs the automated process that gathers the charts every hour. Your browser doesn’t contact GitHub while you use Tenner. See GitHub’s Privacy Statement.
  • TMDB (The Movie Database) supplies the posters, artwork and service logos, which your browser loads directly from TMDB’s image servers. See TMDB’s Privacy Policy.
  • Google provides Sign in with Google. Its button loads from Google only on the Account page, and only Google’s sign-in is involved: if you use it, Google tells us your account number, name, email address and picture. See Google’s Privacy Policy.
  • Resend sends our emails (the link to confirm your email address when you sign up with a password, the link to reset your password, and the Sunday email if you turn it on): it receives your email address and that email. See Resend’s Privacy Policy.
  • YouTube plays trailers. On a computer, a title’s trailer starts by itself (with the sound off) a moment after you open it; on phones, and if you turn off “Start trailers by themselves” in Settings, only when you press Trailer. Tenner uses YouTube’s privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com), and nothing loads from YouTube until a trailer plays. See Google’s Privacy Policy.

The charts and where-to-watch details are gathered by our own automated process from TMDB (with where-to-watch data from JustWatch), Netflix’s published Top 10, Watchmode and Wikidata. Your browser doesn’t contact JustWatch, Netflix, Watchmode or Wikidata while you use Tenner. Watchmode also supplies the Coming soon and Leaving soon dates and the rent and buy prices. If you follow a link to Netflix, TMDB or another site, that site’s own policies apply.

5. Cookies

Tenner doesn’t set any cookies. The full details are on Cookies.

6. Links to other websites

Tenner links to other websites, such as Netflix and TMDB, so you can watch or read more about a title. We aren’t responsible for the content or privacy practices of those sites; please check their own privacy policies.

7. Children

Tenner is a general-audience site. We don’t collect personal information from visitors, including children; only people who choose to make an account, turn on alerts or contact us share anything with us (section 2). Some of the titles shown may not be suitable for younger viewers; check each service’s age ratings, and switch on Hide titles rated 18 in Settings if you’d rather not see them.

8. Your rights

Under UK data protection law (the UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018), you have rights over any personal data we hold about you, including the right to see it, correct it, take a copy or have it deleted. This applies to your account, if you make one (you can see it, change your name and delete it on the Account page), the push address kept for Watchlist alerts, and emails you send us. Otherwise we don’t hold personal data about you. If you have concerns, you can also contact the Information Commissioner’s Office.

9. Changes to this policy

If we change how Tenner handles information, we’ll update this policy and the date at the top before the change takes effect.

10. Contact

Cookies

Updated 17 November 2026

Tenner doesn’t set any cookies, and doesn’t use any third-party cookies for tracking or advertising, so there’s nothing to accept or turn off. The things listed in section 3 of the Privacy Policy are kept in your browser’s own storage on this device, not in cookies.

Accounts

Accounts don’t use cookies either. When you sign in, this device is given a random code that’s kept in your browser’s storage and sent with Tenner’s own requests to its account service, so it knows it’s you; we store only a scrambled copy of it. It isn’t shared with anyone and can’t follow you to other websites. Signing out, or signing a device out from the Account page, makes it stop working straight away.

Sign in with Google

Google’s button only loads on the Account page. Google may use its own cookies, on its own site, to know whether you’re signed in to Google and to keep its sign-in secure. Those are Google’s, under Google’s Privacy Policy; Tenner can’t read them. If you sign in with a passkey or an email and password, Google isn’t involved.

Trailers

Trailers play in YouTube’s privacy-enhanced mode, which doesn’t set cookies until a trailer plays, and nothing loads from YouTube before then.

Cloudflare

Cloudflare, which hosts Tenner, may set a strictly necessary security cookie of its own if it needs to check a visit is from a person rather than an automated program. It isn’t used for tracking or advertising.

Apart from your account and Watchlist alerts, if you choose them, what Tenner keeps on your device never leaves it.

Accessibility

We want Tenner to work well for everyone. It follows your device’s settings:

  • Dark Mode or light, or your own choice of Day, Night or Auto in the Theme menu;
  • Reduce Motion: animations stop, and they can be switched off in Settings › Appearance too;
